Light at the threshold

What does repetition teach the eye?

In music, returning to a passage can reveal a detail missed the first time. This proposed study carries that habit into looking: one room, revisited as daylight moves.

The experiment
Draw the same interior at three times of day. Keep the viewpoint; let colour, shadow, and empty space change.
The artistic decision
Compare what an accurate record captures with what a selective drawing leaves out. Which makes the change easier to feel?
A question to test
Does returning change the image—or change what the observer notices?

Hands that remember

What can a hand show before a sound?

Claire’s nine years of guzheng study give this proposed direction a specific starting point: the hands. Hovering, plucking, pressing, and releasing could become a vocabulary of marks and materials.

The experiment
Begin with Claire’s own observed gestures. Draw the approach, contact, and release as a sequence; translate one movement into repeated marks or layered paper.
The artistic decision
Choose what to preserve: the shape of a hand, the pressure of a mark, or the interval between movements.
A question to test
Can someone who has never played guzheng read a difference between tension and release?

A listening map

Can a pause become something visible?

Guzheng practice offers a starting question about timing: what happens between an action and its release? This proposed interaction would let a visitor explore duration through an image.

Visitor action
Begin a gesture, let it continue, then release it. The visitor chooses the duration.
Visual response
A longer gesture reveals more of an image. Releasing keeps the result visible, so the visitor can compare it with another attempt.
A question to test
Does seeing the trace make a visitor more attentive to the time they spend? Claire would need to make and test her own version to find out.
