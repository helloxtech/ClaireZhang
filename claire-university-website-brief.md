## 1) Executive Summary

Build Claire's site as a **calm, elegant, evidence-first personal portfolio** that supports university applications, scholarship applications, teacher/counsellor references, and interviews.

This should **not** be treated as the main application itself. Official admissions systems at many universities still place the most weight on the required application materials, and some institutions explicitly say they do not review external links or personal websites in key parts of the process. The site therefore needs to do four jobs well:

1. Present Claire as a **serious student with real depth**, not just a list of activities.
2. Showcase her strongest assets in **visual art** and **Guzheng performance**.
3. Make her story easier to translate into official forms, essays, portfolios, résumés, and supplemental submissions.
4. Give teachers, counsellors, scholarship readers, and any admissions reader who *does* click the link a polished, easy-to-understand picture of who she is.

### Best working assumption about grade level
Because Semiahmoo Secondary is a Grade 8–12 school, and students in B.C. typically start university applications in the fall of their Grade 12 year, the most practical working assumption is that Claire is **currently in Grade 11** and would apply in the **2026–27 cycle**. Treat that as an implementation assumption until you confirm her exact grade / grad year.

### Core positioning
Claire should come across as:

- **An artist with discipline and original observation**
- **A musician with sustained training and meaningful community presence**
- **A thoughtful student who reflects on what she learns**
- **A young person whose cultural practice and service actually matter to other people**

### Design thesis in one sentence
The site should feel like a hybrid of a **gallery mini-site**, a **recital program**, and a **high-trust student portfolio**—clean, mature, quiet, and evidence-heavy.

## 2) Hard Truths From Admissions Research

These are the realities that should shape the design.

### 2.1 The website is a support asset, not the main application
Do not build this as if the website itself will carry the application.

Research from official university sources shows:

- Yale says **most successful applicants submit only the required application materials**, and supplements are only for work that is a strong and important part of the application.
- Waterloo says **information in links, including awards or portfolios, will not be reviewed** in its AIF.
- Princeton allows some external media in certain situations, but explicitly says **do not upload links to personal websites** in the arts supplement.
- Stanford Art Practice says **links and URLs will not be reviewed** for interactive work.
- UBC and Yale route important supplements through official application systems, portals, or department-specific submissions.

### What that means for the website
The site should be designed as:

- a **polished external reference**,
- a **content bank** for official application writing,
- a **portfolio companion** for programs that permit links,
- and a **professional identity layer** around the real application.

It should **not** rely on admissions readers spending lots of time browsing.

### 2.2 Curation matters more than volume
Official portfolio guidance repeatedly emphasizes selective, high-quality work, not dumping everything online.

Implication:
- Fewer, stronger pieces beat a big gallery.
- Every page must feel edited.
- Claire should not look “busy”; she should look focused.

### 2.3 Reflection matters, not just accomplishments
UBC's Personal Profile guidance is especially useful here. Their admissions materials emphasize:
- what is important to the student,
- significant achievements,
- what the student learned,
- and challenges overcome.

That means the site should not read like “Claire is talented and accomplished.”  
It should read like: **“Here is what Claire made, why she made it, what she learned, and how she contributed.”**

### 2.4 Non-Western music is a real strength when framed seriously
This is important for Guzheng.

Official sources from Yale and Princeton explicitly welcome non-Western traditions. That means Claire should **not** present Guzheng as an exotic add-on or “cultural extra.” It should be framed as:

- disciplined musicianship,
- sustained training,
- repertoire and interpretation,
- public/community contribution,
- and cultural continuity through performance.

### 2.5 Accessibility and performance are not optional
The site should be clean, fast, captioned, keyboard-usable, and readable on phones. Admissions readers, teachers, and scholarship reviewers may open it on laptops, tablets, or phones under time pressure.

Based on what you shared, the strongest story is **not** simply “Claire does art and music.”

The stronger narrative is:

> Claire is a student who develops patience, sensitivity, discipline, and cultural expression through both visual art and Guzheng performance, and who shares that work with communities in meaningful ways.

That gives Claire a coherent identity instead of two disconnected hobbies.

Painting and Guzheng both require patient practice, attention to detail, technique, and interpretive judgment. This creates a strong “discipline through creativity” story.

Her artwork should be framed not just as “pretty paintings,” but as evidence of how she sees, studies, and thinks.

#### Pillar C — Music as contribution
Her community performances matter because they show that her musicianship is not private only. It is shared with others.

#### Pillar D — Reflection and growth
Each section should include brief reflection:

#### Pillar E — Future curiosity
The website needs one section that looks forward:

This last piece is critical because otherwise the site can feel backward-looking only.

## 4) What To Avoid

This matters as much as what to include.

### Do not make it:
- childish,
- overloaded,
- parent-written,
- braggy,
- decorative without substance,
- or obsessed with awards/certificates.

- auto-playing music,
- too many animations,

- scanned certificates as the main proof of excellence,

- “gifted/talented since childhood” language,

### Important writing rule
The site must sound like **Claire**, not like a school brochure and not like a parent.  
Adults can help edit it, but the voice should remain student-authentic.

## 12) How Claire's Art Page Should Reflect Official Portfolio Trends

The website should be broader than a single university supplement, but its structure should make it easy to adapt.

### UBC Vancouver BFA direct-entry pattern
UBC's first-year BFA Visual Art guidance asks for:
- up to 5 finished artworks,
- at least 2 different media,
- metadata like title/year/dimensions/medium,
- a short description for each work,
- and a written reflection tied to one selected work.

### Yale pattern
Yale Visual Art guidance says:
- 5 to 8 pieces of original work,
- only if visual art is a strong and important part of the application.

### Princeton pattern
Princeton Visual Arts guidance encourages:
- experimentation and imagination over stylistic consistency,
- exploratory / preparatory works such as sketchbook pages,
- and 10–20 images for certain categories.

### Stanford pattern
Stanford Art Practice guidance emphasizes:
- exactly 10 slides in certain media,
- a short artist statement,
- strong ideas and skills,
- independent work,
- and careful metadata structure.

## Design implication
Build the website so the art content can be **reduced easily** into:
- 5-piece pack,
- 8-piece pack,
- 10-piece pack,
- or 10–20 image pack.

Do **not** make Claire's web gallery depend on layouts that break when exported into a formal portfolio list.

## 14) How Claire's Music Page Should Reflect Official Supplement Trends

### Yale music supplement pattern
Yale accepts music of all genres, including non-Western traditions, and advises students to submit only if the work is a strong and important part of the application and shows a high level of ability. Yale Music guidance also recommends:
- two or three contrasting pieces,
- often around ten minutes of best playing,
- clarity over professional production.

### Princeton music pattern
Princeton explicitly includes **Non-Traditional & Non-Western Music** in its music supplement categories. It asks for:
- a résumé of training and accomplishments,
- a personal statement,
- and a 10–15 minute submission that highlights strengths.

### Stanford music pattern
Stanford Music asks instrumentalists to submit:
- 2 or 3 pieces in varying styles/periods/tempi,
- video only,
- unedited,
- natural acoustic,
- full-body frame showing hands and face,
- and no advantage from fancy accompaniment or production.

## Design implication
The public website may show both art and music, but school-specific music materials should be easy to prepare from one clean data source:
- repertoire titles,
- video URLs,
- durations,
- event context,
- training summary,
- résumé,
- musician statement.

## 20) Voice and Writing Rules

All copy should follow these rules.

### Voice characteristics
- first person where appropriate
- reflective
- precise
- modest but confident
- concrete
- not over-edited into adult language

### Sentence style
Prefer:
- specific examples
- simple statements
- short paragraphs
- concrete nouns and verbs

Avoid:
- buzzwords,
- inspirational fluff,
- “passionate about” overuse,
- generic self-praise,

Use this structure repeatedly:
1. What I did / made / performed
2. Why it mattered
3. What I learned
4. What changed in me or my work

That pattern aligns well with UBC-style reflection and also reads well for general admissions.

## 24) Typography Recommendation

Use one serif + one sans-serif.

### Suggested pairing model
- **Display / headings:** refined serif
- **Body / UI:** clean sans-serif

Examples of style, not mandatory exact fonts:
- heading style like Instrument Serif / Cormorant / Newsreader
- body style like Inter / Manrope / Source Sans 3

### Rules
- no more than 2 font families
- generous line-height
- body size around 17–18px desktop
- max text line width around 68–75 characters
